Treatment is a shared plan

Care can combine education, environmental changes, behavioral or psychological support and medication. The mix depends on age, impairment, health, preferences and local guidance. For young children, evidence-based parent training is commonly a first step. Adults may benefit from ADHD-focused psychological treatment, including cognitive behavioral approaches. Treatment aims to reduce difficulties and improve daily functioning; follow-up helps adapt the plan.

Begin with what needs to improve

Practical application

Treatment should address meaningful difficulties in everyday life. Discuss age, other health conditions, preferences and the practical demands of each option. A useful goal is specific enough to review: for example, completing a regular work task with fewer missed steps. Education, environmental changes, psychological or behavioral support and medication may be combined. The plan should be revisited if it is not helping or its demands are unmanageable.

Sources: MedlinePlus: ADHD

Ask what the therapy actually does

Evidence summary

Psychological treatment can help people develop ways of managing difficulties, including patterns of thought and behavior. Ask which approach is proposed, its evidence for your age and needs, what sessions involve and how progress is assessed. For children, parent involvement and behavioral support can be central. For adults, ADHD-focused cognitive behavioral work may address organization and coping. A general promise to “unlock your brain” is not a description of a treatment.

Sources: NIMH: Psychotherapies · NIMH: ADHD — What You Need to Know

Medication needs follow-up, not guesswork

Evidence summary

Benefits and side effects vary between people. A prescriber reviews health and other medicines, explains options and monitors response. Ask which changes should prompt contact and whom to contact between visits. Take prescribed medicines only as directed; never share prescription stimulants or use another person’s supply. Misuse can cause serious harm. Do not copy someone else’s dose or treat a response to a stimulant as proof of ADHD.

Sources: NIMH: Mental Health Medications · FDA: Safe use of prescription stimulants

Be careful with cure claims

Practical application

Natural does not automatically mean safe or effective. Evidence for complementary approaches is variable, and supplements can carry risks or interact with medicines. Ask a qualified professional before adding a product, especially for a child. A balanced routine can support health, but a timer, restrictive diet or supplement should not be presented as a guaranteed ADHD cure. Ask what evidence supports a claim, for whom, and compared with which alternative.

Sources: NCCIH: ADHD and Complementary Health Approaches

A practical next step

Ask what each option could help with, its risks, and how progress will be checked.

Need urgent help?

If you or someone else is in immediate danger or may act on thoughts of self-harm, contact local emergency services now or go to the nearest emergency department. Ask a trusted person to stay with you while you get help. For distress without immediate danger, contact a local qualified health professional promptly. This website is not an emergency service.

Sources: WHO: Suicide questions and answers

Getting an ADHD assessment in Jordan

Finding a service is only the first step. You also need to know who will assess you, what the appointment includes and what it will cost. This guide gives you a way to compare services and prepare questions without assuming that every clinic, insurance plan or referral route works the same way. It is educational guidance, not a diagnosis or a recommendation of a particular clinician.

1. Decide what you are asking for

Write a short description of the problem that led you to look for help: missed work deadlines, repeated school difficulties, losing track of essential tasks, or something else. You do not need to decide your diagnosis before booking. An online score or social-media description can start a conversation, but it cannot settle the diagnosis. There is no single test that establishes ADHD.

When contacting a service in Jordan, say whether the appointment is for an adult, teenager or child. Ask specifically for an ADHD assessment; an appointment advertised as counselling, coaching or general support may have a different purpose. Ask your usual healthcare professional for help finding an appropriate assessment route if you are unsure where to begin.

Background sources: CDC: Diagnosing ADHD · NIMH: Tips for Talking With a Health Care Provider About Your Mental Health

2. Check the person and the place

Ask for the assessor’s full name, professional title, current Jordanian practice-permit details and experience assessing your age group. Also ask who will explain the findings and who would handle medical treatment if it is needed. A title, a directory listing or a foreign qualification alone does not answer these questions.

Jordan’s Ministry of Health Licensing Directorate lists issuing statements about the validity and current status of practice permits among its responsibilities. Its website also provides categories of licensed health facilities. Use these official routes to ask how to check the individual and the facility. A facility listing does not establish the assessment quality of every person working there.

Background sources: Jordan Ministry of Health: responsibilities of the Licensing Directorate · Jordan Ministry of Health: licensed clinics, medical centres and health institutions

3. Compare routes before travelling or paying

For a public-service route, ask your health centre or the relevant hospital whether it can assess ADHD in your age group or direct you elsewhere. Confirm any referral and eligibility requirements with that service. For a private appointment, ask whether the quoted appointment is an initial consultation or the full assessment. These are questions to investigate, not a promise that either route is available to every reader.

If you live outside Amman or would need time off work, confirm the exact location, number of visits and whether any preparation can be completed remotely. For an online appointment, ask whether the provider can assess someone physically located in Jordan, what an in-person examination might involve, and how follow-up would work.

4. Ask for a complete cost explanation

We have not verified a standard assessment price, insurance benefit or waiting time for Jordan. Ask the service and your insurer separately before paying. Keep the answers together so that two offers can be compared on what they actually include. A low first-visit fee may tell you little about the total.

Use this booking checklist. “Not yet confirmed” is a useful answer to record; it is a reason to ask again before committing, not a space to fill with an estimate.

  • What is charged for consultation, assessment, questionnaires and the written report?
  • Are follow-up and treatment-planning appointments separate?
  • Does insurance need prior approval, a referral or a particular provider?
  • What are the payment, cancellation and rescheduling terms?

5. Expect a history, not just a score

An adult assessment considers childhood history as well as current difficulties. School records or information from someone who knows you may help. Tell the clinician if records are unavailable, if memories are uncertain, or if involving family would be difficult. Do not invent details to make your account sound complete.

The UK Adult ADHD Network’s assessment standard recommends addressing missing or conflicting information and explaining how it affects conclusions. It is a useful discussion aid, not Jordanian law. Ask how the assessor will explore the information available to you and consider other possible explanations. You can request clarification without trying to steer the assessment toward a particular result.

Background sources: NIMH: ADHD in Adults — 4 Things to Know · UK Adult ADHD Network: The adult ADHD assessment quality assurance standard (2024)

6. A message you can adapt

This is a fictional booking example, not a patient testimonial or an endorsement of a clinic:

“Hello. I am looking for an ADHD assessment for an adult in Jordan. Who conducts it, and what is their professional title and practice-permit information? What does the assessment include, what is the total expected cost, and is a written report included? I do not have my school reports. Can you explain how that is handled? Please also confirm the location, language of the appointment and earliest available date.”

Keep the first message limited to booking questions. Ask for the service’s approved method before sending medical records, identification documents or details about someone else. If you need a report for a school, employer or another clinician, ask that recipient what documentation it accepts before paying for extra paperwork.

7. Leave with a usable next step

After the assessment, ask for the conclusion in plain language, any remaining uncertainty and the next action. If more information or another assessment is needed, clarify who arranges it. If ADHD is not diagnosed, ask what else may explain the difficulties and what support is appropriate. Before leaving, write down one agreed action, who is responsible and when you will hear back. The result should help you understand what happens next, even when the answer is not yet complete.

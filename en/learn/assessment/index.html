What an assessment involves

There is no single blood test, scan or online quiz that establishes ADHD. A qualified clinician gathers a developmental and health history, discusses symptoms and their impact across settings, and considers other explanations and coexisting conditions. Questionnaires may support this work but cannot establish a diagnosis alone. With permission, information from someone who knows you well or old school reports may help. Referral routes differ by country.

A conversation about the whole picture

Evidence summary

An ADHD assessment explores how attention, activity and impulse control have developed over time and how they affect daily life. A clinician asks about childhood as well as the present, home as well as school or work, and difficulties as well as strengths. The aim is to understand a pattern, not to reward the highest symptom score. Ask who will assess you and what ADHD training and professional registration they hold.

Sources: UKAAN: Adult ADHD assessment quality assurance standard

What information may be useful

Practical application

Bring two or three specific examples: what happened, how often, and what the consequences were. Note when difficulties first became noticeable. Available school reports and information from people who know you can add context; ask the clinic how consent and confidentiality are handled. If childhood records are unavailable, say so rather than inventing a history. Ask how the clinician will explore the information that is available.

Sources: NIMH: Talking with a health care provider

Check other explanations too

Evidence summary

Anxiety, depression, sleep difficulties and some learning problems can produce similar experiences. They can also coexist with ADHD. The assessment may include a physical examination, hearing or vision checks, and other investigations when indicated. Describe all medicines, supplements, sleep problems and recent changes in health. A computer task or questionnaire can contribute information, but no single result establishes the diagnosis by itself.

Sources: MedlinePlus: ADHD Screening

Leave with an understandable next step

Practical application

Ask the clinician to explain their conclusion, remaining uncertainties and recommended support. Useful questions include: “What best explains my difficulties?”, “What should we address first?” and “When will we review progress?” You can request a written summary and ask how to seek clarification or another opinion. If ADHD is not diagnosed, the difficulties that brought you there still deserve attention; ask what further assessment or support is appropriate.

Sources: UKAAN: Adult ADHD assessment quality assurance standard · NIMH: Talking with a health care provider

A practical next step

Ask a licensed healthcare professional about an assessment with someone trained in ADHD.

Need urgent help?

If you or someone else is in immediate danger or may act on thoughts of self-harm, contact local emergency services now or go to the nearest emergency department. Ask a trusted person to stay with you while you get help. For distress without immediate danger, contact a local qualified health professional promptly. This website is not an emergency service.

Sources: WHO: Suicide questions and answers

Preparing for your first ADHD appointment

A useful preparation sheet helps you describe your difficulties and remember your questions. It does not need to prove that you have ADHD. Use the prompts below for yourself or adapt them with a child or teenager, keeping their own experience central.

1. Make one page, not a perfect life history2. Describe events the clinician can explore3. Add a short timeline and mark uncertainty4. Bring the health information that changes the picture5. Plan for language, privacy and support6. Copy this appointment worksheet7. Record the plan before you leave

Published by ADHDJO · Owner: Nabil Aref · Updated 19 November 2026.
AI-assisted research, drafting and translation. Independent clinical review has not been completed. How this content is prepared

1. Make one page, not a perfect life history

Your first task is to make your main concern easy to find. Write why you booked and the two or three difficulties you most want help understanding. You may use Arabic, English or both in your own notes; ask the clinic beforehand which language the appointment and any questionnaires will use. Short notes are enough to start. This worksheet has no score and cannot diagnose ADHD.

If preparing feels overwhelming, complete only the first three lines: “I booked because…”, “The hardest part of my week is…”, and “I hope this appointment will help me…”. Bring the unfinished page. You can ask the clinician to help you explain something rather than delaying the visit until every box is filled.

2. Describe events the clinician can explore

Instead of relying only on labels such as “lazy”, “forgetful” or “bad at school”, choose specific situations. Note what happened, where it happened, how often you noticed it and its practical effect. Include occasions when the same task went more smoothly and what was different. The purpose is an accurate picture, not a persuasive case for one diagnosis.

Fictional example: “This month I missed two bill deadlines after putting the letters in a drawer. Both led to late fees. When the due date was on a visible calendar, I paid on time.” This is an example of useful description. The event by itself does not establish ADHD.

3. Add a short timeline and mark uncertainty

Divide your notes into childhood, school or further education, and adult life where relevant. Write one remembered example for each period, or “I do not remember”. Adult ADHD assessment considers earlier history; available school reports and information from someone who knew you may help. You do not need to manufacture a date or copy someone else’s symptom story.

If reports are missing, note where you have already looked and tell the clinic. If a parent is unavailable, or asking family would be distressing, explain that privately. The clinician needs to decide how to assess the available evidence. A relative’s presence should not be promised in advance without first discussing consent and the clinic’s process.

Background sources: NIMH: ADHD in Adults — 4 Things to Know

4. Bring the health information that changes the picture

Prepare a list of medicines, vitamins, supplements and non-prescription products you use, including dose if known. Bring previous relevant reports if you have them. Note major recent changes, sleep difficulties and any other concerns you want to discuss. NIMH recommends preparing medication lists and questions before a mental-health appointment.

For a child, ask the clinic what school observations it wants and how they should be obtained. CDC describes assessment across settings and consideration of other explanations. A child’s difficult week, or an adult’s concentration problem, should be explored in context. Do not change prescribed medication simply to prepare for this appointment; ask the prescribing clinician about any instructions.

Background sources: NIMH: Tips for Talking With a Health Care Provider About Your Mental Health · CDC: Diagnosing ADHD

5. Plan for language, privacy and support

Ask about appointment length, breaks, accessibility and whether a trusted person can join for part of the discussion. Decide what you would prefer to say alone. If an interpreter is needed, ask the clinic about arrangements rather than assuming a family member must translate sensitive information. For a child or teenager, ask how the young person’s own perspective will be heard.

Before sending forms, ask who can access them, how they are stored and how information from relatives or school will be requested. Ask the clinician to explain confidentiality and its limits in your situation. This guide does not state a blanket privacy rule or promise how a particular Jordanian clinic handles records.

6. Copy this appointment worksheet

Keep the worksheet with you and your care team. There is no need to submit it to ADHDJO. Leave any question blank that you would rather discuss face to face. These prompts are an original organizational aid, not a validated screening tool.

  • My main question and the change I hope for:
  • Two recent examples: situation / what happened / frequency / effect / what helped.
  • Earlier examples or records I have; information I am unsure about:
  • Medicines, supplements, other health concerns and recent changes:
  • People or records that may add context; my preferences about involving them:
  • My three questions for the clinician:
  • Practical arrangements still to confirm: language / location / total cost / report / follow-up.

7. Record the plan before you leave

Use a second small box for the outcome: what the clinician thinks so far; what remains uncertain; any next assessment; and the next agreed action. Ask when and how you will receive a written summary, if one is being prepared, and whom to contact if you do not understand it. For any proposed treatment, ask what it is intended to help, possible risks, alternatives and how progress will be reviewed.

Before the appointment ends, read back the next step in your own words: “So I will arrange X, your clinic will send Y, and we will speak again on Z—is that right?” If the answer is not yet known, record who will find out. You should not have to reconstruct the whole plan from memory on the journey home.
